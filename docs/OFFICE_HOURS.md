# Office Hours — ChannelArk

**Mode:** Startup, pre-product. **Status:** diagnostic complete from report evidence; assumptions remain unvalidated.

## Six forcing answers
1. **Demand reality:** People pay thousands after loss and agencies buy backup/protection. That proves pain can command money, not that they will prepay. Missing proof: a paid preventive audit.
2. **Status quo:** platform exports + cheap backup + folders + staff memory + appeals. It protects files inconsistently and not revenue continuity, sponsor evidence, or owned audience access.
3. **Desperate specificity:** agency operations lead managing 5–50 creators. A multi-account suspension causes client churn, sponsor failure, and reputational damage.
4. **Narrowest paid wedge:** one $1,500 roster audit with a measured 72-hour drill—not an archive platform or insurance product.
5. **Observation plan:** watch three ops leads assemble an incident packet without help. Record where they search, what is missing, time-to-standby, and off-platform contact ability.
6. **Future-fit:** AI-first enforcement and fragmented revenue make continuity more important. Reliable human appeals and portable exports would weaken the premise.

## Premise challenge
- The outcome is **continued revenue after platform loss**, not “having a backup.”
- The audit matters only if it exposes a gap the buyer acts on and shortens recovery.
- Recovery is not guaranteed; never imply privileged platform access.
- Start manually. Continuous ingestion before paid audits inverts learning.

## Approaches
- **A — Manual audit kit (minimal):** S effort, low risk. Fast paid learning; labor-heavy.
- **B — Readiness workbench (recommended demo):** M effort, medium risk. Deterministic risk model, evidence gaps, drill plan, human validation.
- **C — Continuous archive + insurer network (ideal):** XL effort, high risk. Defensible after proof; premature now.

## Recommendation and assignment
Build B as a demo, sell A first. Contact 30 US creator agencies, offer a free roster score, and ask the five most exposed to pay $1,500 for a facilitated drill. Success: at least three paid commitments within 14 days. Interest and waitlists do not count.

Continue only if buyers treat drill failure as urgent. Expand after ten paid audits reveal repeatable evidence gaps. Explore insurance distribution only if a broker confirms the artifact changes underwriting or renewal behavior.
