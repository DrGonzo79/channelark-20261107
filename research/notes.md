## Buyer and painful status quo
The beachhead buyer is a creator agency or management company with 5–50 professional creators, each earning at least $5K/month and leaning on one or two platforms. Today, operators use platform-native exports, cheap backup tools, scattered drives, improvised appeal documents, and post-incident recovery vendors. Those pieces do not prove that content, customer access, sponsor records, or an owned fallback channel can actually be restored.

A suspension can erase audience access, sponsor evidence, and revenue in a day. The report cites large 2026 enforcement volumes and creators describing sudden loss of high-value channels. These examples establish severity, not incidence for this ICP.

## Wedge and market gap
The narrow paid wedge is a **$1,500 recovery-readiness audit** for one agency roster. It inventories platform concentration, archive completeness, portable contact access, standby-site readiness, sponsor evidence, and drill recency; then runs a measured recovery drill and produces a dated continuity certificate.

This sits between cheap content backup ($19–$119/month), post-incident recovery ($360 to several thousand dollars), and liability/cyber insurance that usually pays after a covered loss. The gap is not “more backup.” It is tested, creator-owned business continuity with evidence an agency can show a sponsor or underwriter.

## Why now and demand signals
- Reported 2026 enforcement is at industrial scale: YouTube 2.73M channel removals in Q2 and Meta more than 10M Instagram removals during the year.
- Recovery search demand is large: “facebook account recovery” 135K/month (+49% YoY), “account recovery” 201K/month (+22%), “facebook recovery” 60.5K/month, “recover disabled facebook account” 22.2K/month.
- Prevention-language search is reportedly near zero. This invalidates SEO-first launch and forces agency sales.
- Adjacent insurance and creator-spend figures suggest budget, but do not prove demand for this preventive service.
- Public complaints show livelihood impact. They are directional evidence, not representative sampling.

## Competitor map
- **SocialGuardian / Bitig:** broad platform backup; incumbent and price anchor.
- **TubeBuddy:** YouTube metadata export, not continuity.
- **SafeWord / CFC / CreatorSure:** creator liability/cyber-adjacent insurance; possible partners.
- **Essentras / Social Retrieving:** post-incident recovery; validate spend but carry scam/trust tax.
- **Status quo:** native exports, Drive, spreadsheets, staff memory, platform appeal forms.

## Offer ladder and economics
1. Free 10-minute Recovery Readiness Scorecard.
2. $1,500 one-time audit and first drill.
3. $99–$149 per creator/month continuity retainer; lower end at 20+ seats.
4. Separate $2,500–$7,500 incident recovery only after trust/legal boundaries.

Report napkin math assumes 1,500 cold emails, 8% replies, 50% reply-to-demo, 20% demo-to-close, 12 agencies, 8 creators/agency, and $120/creator/month. Those conversions are unvalidated. It models $11,520 month-12 MRR plus audits and incident work for roughly $268K year-one revenue.

## GTM and risks
Lead with a finite evidence-producing audit for US agencies/MCNs, not “account recovery.” Score one roster, ask for a paid drill, and measure replies, paid acceptance, drill time, and uncovered single points of failure. Broker distribution is an expansion, not day-one dependency.

Risks: platforms soften enforcement; API/export limits; recovery-category credibility; sensitive creator data; no prevention search; and a likely lifestyle-business ceiling without insurance distribution.
